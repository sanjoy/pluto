# `beseem`: an unusual generated word that is present in training text

The revised goal is satisfied by **beseem**, emitted during the actual
continuation of `to be or not to be`. Unlike the earlier `shagemper` example,
this word occurs in the supplied training text. It is not a single GPT-2
vocabulary entry: the actual generated pieces are **` b` + `ese` + `em`**.

The mechanism is distributed across three forward passes. The initial ` b` is
a non-greedy sample; `ese` becomes the leading intermediate readout after the
sixth block's MLP, and `em` first leads after the first block's MLP. Native
removals show strong dependence on that first MLP, but do not establish a unique
word-storage location. All block numbers below are **zero-based**.

## Why this word qualifies

[Merriam-Webster labels beseem archaic](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/beseem):
it is a lexical verb meaning to be fitting or suitable. It is not merely an
ordinary word split into BPE tokens or a routine plural/comparative/tense form.
The native fragments ` b`, `ese`, `em` are not three ordinary words making a
conventional phrase. Its historical morphology does not make modern `be seem`
an ordinary equivalent expression. This is a bounded lexical-commonness
assessment, not a claim that the word has no morphological derivation.

The exact tokenizer has no case-insensitive whole-word entry for `beseem`,
either bare or with a leading space. All three emitted IDs are nevertheless
valid vocabulary tokens. The word is complete: its next generated token is
` been`, providing the right delimiter.

There are **six** standalone occurrences in the source corpus. **Five lie in
the configured training partition**, before the line-aligned 90/10 split at
byte 4,892,836; one lies in the evaluation suffix. Every occurrence uses the
same native three-ID sequence as generation. For example:

> O let it then as well beseem thy heart

### The sampling operation

Production computes `exp(double(float32(logit - maximum)) / 0.8)`, normalizes
with a sequential double sum, and samples from a token-ID-ordered CDF using
`mt19937(seed + 1)`, hence RNG seed 18. For these noninitial CDF intervals the
lower endpoint is exclusive and the upper endpoint inclusive.

| Piece | Recorded U | CDF lower | CDF upper |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ` b` | 0.573117240101466 | 0.45971327402808765 | 0.5906012004825171 |
| `ese` | 0.9882889225012688 | 0.012244316139990954 | 0.9944110061709697 |
| `em` | 0.12912533685530286 | 0.00008773078048968204 | 0.9996980180344687 |

Each draw is inside its token's interval. In particular, ` b` is selected
despite comma having the larger logit: 9.556323051452637 versus
10.554195404052734. The model did not choose ` b` by argmax. All 2,048 sampled
events, 4,096 underlying MT words, full RNG states, and 1,029 context-window
slides were independently audited.

## Layer trace: visibility and causal dependence are different

The diagnostic applies the model's actual final LayerNorm and tied output head
to each of 17 earlier residual streams. These are native GPU readouts, not a
separately trained probe. The [complete tables](CORPUS_PRESENT_WORD_READOUTS.md)
show probabilities and ranks without hiding temporary reversals.

- **` b`:** never top-ranked in these readouts. Its probability rises from
  0.0294% after B7 attention to 13.0888% after B7 MLP; sampling picks rank 2.
- **`ese`:** first becomes top-ranked at **B5 MLP**, rising from 0.9382% after
  B5 attention to 55.8337%; final probability is 98.2167%.
- **`em`:** first becomes top-ranked at **B0 MLP** (55.7736%), loses that
  position in B1, regains it at B4 MLP (75.6712%), loses it again after B5
  attention, then wins from B5 MLP onward; final probability is 99.9610%.

All 16 whole-branch and 64 individual-head removals were run separately for
each of the three word pieces, for **240 native intervention forwards**.

| Removal | `ese` probability | `em` probability |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| None | 98.2167% | 99.9610% |
| B0 MLP | 0.0000217% | 0.0159439% |
| B4 MLP | 12.2794% | 94.6249% |
| B5 MLP | 45.7006% | 98.7908% |

The first MLP is a strong dependency in this context, even though `ese` is not
readable as the leading token immediately after it. Removing an entire branch
affects all prefix positions and downstream computation. These tests are
therefore not evidence that B0 uniquely stores this word, nor that the first
top-ranked intermediate readout is a unique causal introduction point.

Probability must also be distinguished from one fixed-competitor margin:
removing B0 MLP actually improves `ese`'s margin over its original competitor,
apostrophe, while collapsing its probability because other vocabulary entries
dominate. The complete changed distributions are preserved.

## Analytical operations and concrete weights

Each of the eight blocks performs pre-LayerNorm, Q/K/V projections, causal
attention `softmax(Q K^T / 8) V`, attention output projection and residual add,
then another LayerNorm, a 512→2,048 projection, tanh-GELU, a 2,048→512
projection, and another residual add. The final LayerNorm feeds the tied
50,257-entry output head. Learned absolute position embeddings are included.

The analysis uses captured native BF16 activations, BF16-rounded matrix
operands, FP32 biases/normalization parameters, epsilon 1e-5, and the actual
FP32 GELU constants. FP64 analytical contractions are **not** silently treated
as the GPU's reduction order: conversion and accumulation remainders remain
explicit at every stage.

For each actual target t and a fixed competitor c, let

```text
w = E_BF16[t] - E_BF16[c]
s = sqrt(mean((actual_final_residual - mean(actual_final_residual))^2) + 1e-5)
u = gamma_final * w / s
u = u - mean(u)
```

Every captured residual component is projected onto u. Token and position
embeddings, all attention heads, all 16,384 MLP neurons, biases, residual
rounding, final-LayerNorm remainder, and output-head accumulation remainder
sum to the **actual sampled token's logit margin**. This linearizes only the
final readout for accounting; the intervening transformer is still nonlinear.

The three native margins are -0.9978723526000977 for ` b` versus comma,
+3.7368736267089844 for `ese` versus apostrophe, and +7.29573917388916 for
`em` versus `eming`. Independent scalar/array reconstruction closes all four
traced margins, including the boundary token, within 3.0e-15.

### Worked example: B5 neuron 424 producing support for `ese`

At context row 241, this neuron's native LayerNorm input is multiplied by W1
column 424 and its bias added. The precise checkpoint addresses are:

- `weight_70.bin`: 512 W1 elements, first byte 1,696, stride 8,192 bytes.
- `weight_71.bin`: FP32 bias at byte 1,696, value -0.0223383828997612.
- `weight_72.bin`: 512-element W2 row, bytes [868,352,870,400).

The analytical pre-GELU sum is **2.363748485222459**; the actual native
pre-GELU value is **2.359375**. Applying the implemented tanh-GELU and native
rounding yields the captured output **2.34375**. The BF16 W2 row's dot product
with the final readout direction u is **0.36971162494943277**. Thus:

```text
direct ese-versus-apostrophe contribution
  = native_GELU * dot(W2_row_BF16, u)
  = 2.34375 * 0.36971162494943277
  = +0.866511620975233
```

This is a measured numerical contribution, not a claimed effect of removing
that individual neuron. Positive and opposing neurons are both included in
the full accounting. Another addressed example, B0 neuron 1065, supplies
+0.376736321737061 toward `em` versus `eming`: native pre-GELU 1.421875,
post-GELU 1.3125, and output-row dot u 0.2870371975139512.

Attention is also expanded into individual source-position contributions. For
`ese`, B4 head 3 contributes +1.158267859949833 to the margin; the source
` much` at context position 240 receives reconstructed attention
0.2728170476462599 and supplies +0.2813636461295334. This is contextual
computation, not an isolated lookup of the spelling. The reconstruction's
BF16 context discrepancy and source-sum remainder are retained in the evidence.
